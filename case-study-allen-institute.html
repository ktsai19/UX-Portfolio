Case study 04 · Allen Institute for AI · Semantic Scholar

Helping researchers quickly and effectively find the papers they need

Semantic Scholar uses AI and NLP to help researchers discover relevant scientific literature. I translated its most complex backend features — paper classification, AI-matched authorship — into interfaces researchers could trust at a glance.

Role

Product Designer

Timeline

6 months, 2020 – 2021

Team

Product, Engineering, Research, Data

Tools

Figma, Photoshop, Principle

Overview

Semantic Scholar uses AI to help scholars discover relevant research. My role was translating complex AI and NLP features into user-friendly design — across search, new features, and content explainability — with product, engineering, research, and data teams.

The problem

With 190M+ papers, finding the right one fast is hard. And as the platform started using AI to match papers to author profiles, authors needed a way to review those matches, catch mistakes, and trust the result.

Goals & constraints

Goal

Help users browse and evaluate thousands of research papers more effectively

Goal

Let authors quickly review AI-matched papers, including low-confidence predictions

Constraint

Stay legible inside an already dense, text-heavy paper snippet UI

Process

Two related problems, one thread: help people trust an AI-assisted system fast enough to actually use it.

  • Paper badges. Interviewed 5 researchers, then A/B tested 3 placements — icons beat shorthand letters, and placement had to match natural scanning patterns.
  • Four design principles. Extensible, responsive, harmonious, and discernible shaped every badge decision.
  • AI-matched approval. Authors needed final say on low-confidence matches, a fast review flow, and a feedback loop to improve the model.
  • Simplified by testing. Usability studies showed a dedicated review space beat a cluttered settings-page approach.

Outcome

The badging system shipped live on Semantic Scholar, giving users an at-a-glance way to assess a paper's type and credibility — and drew positive internal feedback for brightening up a page that had been text-only. The paper approval feature shipped with a transparent, built-in feedback loop so authors could correct false matches and improve the underlying model over time, alongside a restructured search bar that made citation search discoverable without taking up permanent space.
